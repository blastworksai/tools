# ROCKETFUEL — a founder-led collaboration protocol

Adapted from *Rocket Fuel* by Gino Wickman and Mark C. Winters.
The protocol separates direction, planning, execution, and review so that AI-assisted work stays bounded and independently verified.

## Seats

### Founder / Owner

The Founder sets direction, priorities, constraints, and the final call. Their vision should be captured in their own words rather than paraphrased into a different goal.

Execution begins only with the Founder's explicit approval. This is a structural gate, not an exception for unusually risky work.

### Visionary

The Visionary owns:

- Planning and sequencing.
- Standards and executable acceptance criteria.
- Scope and intent review.
- Review of results against the agreed standard.

The Visionary may propose, challenge, and review, but does not silently take over execution.

### Integrator

The Integrator owns:

- Idea filtering and shiny-object protection.
- Cadence and execution tie-breaks.
- Building inside the approved scope and sandbox.
- Calling bounded specialist support.
- Reporting results and evidence.

The Integrator may suggest changes to the plan; the Founder and Visionary retain their respective decision rights.

### Seat rules

- Authority belongs to the assigned seat, not to a model, product, or tool.
- A person or agent may occupy different seats in different tasks, but an assignment must be explicit.
- Reassigning one seat does not automatically move another actor.
- A vacated seat remains vacant until explicitly filled.
- An override replaces the current assignment for its stated scope; it does not accumulate with it. The actor returns to an unassigned state afterward.
- Specialist profiles are task-scoped and do not acquire standing authority from the seat that calls them.

## Two tiers

### Organization

At the organization tier, the Visionary holds planning, standards, acceptance criteria, and review. The Integrator holds filtering, cadence, building, execution tie-breaks, and reporting. The Founder retains direction and final authority.

### Delivery team

Relative to delegated specialists, the dispatching seat writes the specification and contract, then delegates execution. Results still receive independent review against the Visionary's standard.

Deterministic plumbing belongs in scripts rather than model delegation. Do not hand-build work that a bounded specialist was dispatched to perform.

## Independent review and attribution

- The reviewer must not be the author of the work being reviewed.
- The reviewer independently reproduces decisive evidence before approval, merge, migration, release, or deployment.
- Pasted output is a lead, not proof. Access to an artifact is not verification of it.
- Attribution follows the actor that actually performed the action, not a self-declared role.
- A self-review must remain visibly labeled as a self-review.
- A review that cannot observe the required artifact or execution remains unapproved and names exactly what could not be observed.
- Support specialists have no standing authority beyond their bounded dispatch.

## The Founder's two gates

1. **Explicit dispatch:** execution begins only on the Founder's explicit go.
2. **Capability-complete notification:** when a coherent capability is complete, notify the Founder for human review. Finishing a single task is not necessarily completing a capability.

Neither gate relaxes the other. The Founder has the final call.

## Runtime context

Session context, task links, and remembered obligations can point to current work, but they are not proof of workflow state. Completion, approval, release, and deployment require current source-of-truth checks and their normal gates.

## Adversarial tiers

Rocket Fuel is **invoke-only**. On invocation, recommend **BASIC** or **FULL** with a short rationale and ask the Operator which tier to use. The Operator's selection binds that invocation. The skill never changes tiers or adds rounds on its own; a material new risk goes back to the Operator as a fresh decision.

### BASIC

- Run at most **two rounds**: the strategist proposes, the builder attacks, and the strategist amends for the second attack. Each attack must be narrower than the previous one.
- Stop immediately on convergence.
- Without convergence after round 2, stop and surface both positions and the exact disagreement to the Founder for intervention.
- There is no third BASIC round and no automatic promotion to FULL.

### FULL — the convergence protocol

- **Rounds 1-3:** the strategist proposes and amends; the builder attacks. Each attack must be narrower than the previous one. Stop immediately on convergence.
- **After three NO-SHIPs, swap seats for up to two more rounds.** The previous builder authors a complete alternative honoring every locked Founder ruling; the previous strategist becomes the adversary.
- Without convergence after round 5, return both positions and the exact disagreement to the Operator. The Operator may **extend** with additional rounds or **overrule** by directing the outcome. Extension does not require a new Founder ruling.
- Founder rulings remain fixed throughout every round and role swap unless the Operator expressly overrules them.

The Builder is always free to SUGGEST; the Integrator filters. **Authorship is symmetric; termination is not.** Both seats propose and attack, but the Operator controls the tier, every extension, and every overrule. The Integrator's personal proof-run is required at every tier and sits outside these round budgets, as do all other independent proof, review, and deployment gates.

Record every round's critique, including rejected points and the reasons for rejecting them. Silently rejecting every critique and accepting every critique both destroy the value of independent review.

## Issue processing

1. **Identify:** state the root issue in one sentence.
2. **Discuss:** make each point once; repetition is not additional evidence.
3. **Solve:** decide who owns the action before deciding the detailed method.

Turn solutions into actions that can be verified in the next cycle. Keep each cycle to three to seven attainable priorities; more than that removes meaningful prioritization.

## Dispatch mechanics

Every dispatch should be bounded and reproducible:

- Give the executor a written destination, scope, acceptance criteria, authority, tool limits, and finish line.
- Use the least-privileged sandbox appropriate to the task. Set network and filesystem permissions explicitly.
- State sandbox and permission settings on both initial dispatch and resume; never assume a resumed session inherited safe settings.
- Give every dispatch a hard timeout and a defined stop condition.
- Ensure non-interactive invocations receive end-of-file on standard input rather than waiting indefinitely for input.
- Keep dispatches that share mutable state sequential unless parallel execution has been explicitly designed and authorized.
- Record which model or runtime performed the work when that affects provenance or reproducibility.
- Preserve the argument transcript: every critique, accepted change, rejected point, and rejection reason.
- Split claims into **verified in this session** and **unverified information from the model or memory**.
- Test network restrictions early when network access is material to the task.
- Verify by inspecting the output artifact and the process state. Do not treat elapsed time or the exit status of a compound command as sufficient proof.

A dispatch grants only the authority written into it. It does not implicitly grant approval, merge, migration, deployment, publishing, spending, or access to secrets.
